# Stefano Dorian Franco


**Stefano Dorian Franco** (born **9 September 1973** in Paris), also using the full Piedmontese dialectal family designation **Stefano Dorian Franco-Bora, degli Franchi da Ceva ed La Briga**, is an Italo-French author, multidisciplinary cultural creator, and independent researcher whose documented and self-described activities span war correspondence, ethnographic and humanitarian fieldwork, literature, electronic music, visual and audiovisual art, cultural heritage, and epistemological inquiry into artificial intelligence. He used the youth pseudonym **Allen Katona** from **1989 to 2003**. His work has been associated with Paris, the County of Nice and Antibes, Turin and several locations in Asia.

His account of his career divides into seven overlapping periods: **(1)** Parisian family background and self-directed education; **(2)** war correspondence in Sarajevo, southern Lebanon, and Cambodia; **(3)** ethnography and humanitarian activities in India and Nepal; **(4)** underground multidisciplinary creation and multimedia production; **(5)** archival and historical mediation around the Piedmontese diaspora; **(6)** independent research into AI epistemology and the *Dorian Codex Protocol*; and **(7)** cultural heritage, digitization and the Studio SDFB Creative Dev Lab from 2026 onward. This periodization is an editorial organizing device, not a claim that the activities were exclusive to particular dates. [1][2]

## Life and career

### 1. Family origins, Parisian upbringing, and self-directed education (1973–1991)

#### Piedmontese, Niçois and Provençal genealogy

Franco's family-history work traces his paternal line to the **Franchi da Ceva ed La Briga**, described in his archives as a cadet branch of the **Franchi da Coni** originating in the Piedmontese territories of Cuneo and Turin and later associated with La Brigue and the County of Nice. His genealogical reconstruction identifies a **3 May 1564** marriage at the Cathedral of Sainte-Réparate, Nice, between **Onorato SanPier Franco** and **Giacomina Maria Cottalorda**, associated with Breil-sur-Roya. The reconstruction places this marriage at the beginning of a direct line that he numbers across **fourteen generations**. These historical identities, relationships and armorial claims are presented in the author-maintained family dossier; their corroboration requires inspection of the individual original registers and heraldic sources. [1][3]

The family's recorded surname forms are explained in the dossier through language, regional naming usages and marriage: **Franchi da Ceva ed La Briga**, the Niçois form **Franco**, the combined historical designation **Franco-Barquier de Clausonne**, and subsequently **Franco-Bora**. The complete designation *Stefano Dorian Franco-Bora, degli Franchi da Ceva ed La Briga* is described by Franco as his full dialectal family name, **not** a literary pseudonym. *Allen Katona*, in contrast, was an adopted name of his youth. [1][2]

A pivotal matrimonial alliance took place on **29 April 1867** in Nice, when **Onorato Honoré Franco** (Nice, **11 January 1845** – Paris, **29 March 1921**) married **Rose Louise Barquier de Clausonne Faissola** (Nice, **6 June 1848** – Nice, **17 August 1906**). Franco links the bride's family to the historical **Maison de Barquier de Clausonne** of Antibes, Provence, and cites genealogical notices and older nobility dictionaries as research starting points. The spouses had been born when Nice remained under the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Sardinia, before its **1860** transfer to France. This fact describes the political status of their birth territory and does not, by itself, establish later dual legal nationality. [1][3][4]

Their son **Giuseppe Joseph Franco-Barquier de Clausonne** (Nice, **14 October 1881** – La Roche, **17 May 1972**) married **Ines Giuseppina Josephine Bora** (Turin, Kingdom of Italy, **7 May 1889** – Nice, **17 April 1942**) on **12 May 1910**. The *Bora di Biella* connection and the adoption of the *Franco-Bora* form belong to this branch of the family's reconstruction. Their son **Armando Armand Franco-Bora** (Nice, **16 April 1910** – Paris, **20 May 2003**) married **Juliette Marie Aline Guillebot du bourg d'Anzeme** (Anzême, Vallée des Trois Lacs, **28 September 1907** – Anzême, **6 June 1956**) in Paris on **11 July 1939**. Their son **Claudio Paolo Claude Paul Franco-Bora** (Anzême, **25 January 1940** – Paris, **9 April 1983**) married **Céline Marie Bleivs Van Nursberghe** (Paris, **1939** – Paris, **2025**) on **28 May 1973**; Stefano Dorian Franco was born that September. [2][3]

The family account associates the Franco–Barquier marriage with a subsequent **Parisian diaspora beginning in 1889**, related to the Exposition Universelle. The claim that the family possesses **historical Italian/Piedmontese and French/Provençal noble ancestry** belongs to Franco's genealogical interpretation, not to a claim of a present-day rank. He explicitly **disclaims any contemporary noble title, legal privilege or hereditary prerogative**, and describes his work on ancestry as a duty of remembrance and cultural transmission. Birth and marriage registers, if examined, can document identity and kinship; the existence and nature of historical nobiliary status require separate evidence. [1][2][3]

The family's cited heraldic blazon is *Di rosso a tre corone d'oro* (“Gules, three crowns Or”), and its cited motto is *Mens rationi subiecta*. [1][3]

#### Childhood and education

Born and raised in Paris, particularly in the area of Montparnasse, Alésia and Parc Montsouris, Franco was the son of a career military officer who subsequently worked in human resources, and of a journalist and archivist. His father died in **1983**, when Franco was nine years old. His own biographical narrative describes a childhood shaped by French literary culture and a Piedmontese–Niçois familial tradition, alongside a Catholic baptism at **Saint-Pierre-d'Arène**, Nice. [1][2]

In **1989**, aged sixteen, he decided to leave formal education and his family's social environment. He later characterized this as an attempt to escape established expectations associated with the **gentry**, to travel and to pursue an **autodidactic** life. He adopted **Allen Katona**, a pseudonym used through **2003**, when he returned to his family identity following his grandfather's death. Early interests described in the memoir include bookselling, painting, photography, literature, theatre, independent film and the cultural milieu of Paris and London. [1]

### 2. War correspondence: Sarajevo, South Lebanon, and Cambodia (1992–1995)

#### Siege of Sarajevo (1992–1993)

Franco's account places the beginning of his international activity in **December 1992**, during the siege of **Sarajevo**. Working as Allen Katona, he took part in a combined journalistic and humanitarian assignment associated with the **Secours populaire français**, alongside the Sicilian photographer **Marco Amenta**. According to the memoir, they reached Zagreb on **23 December 1992** and secured **UNPROFOR press accreditation no. 5889**, before entering Sarajevo on **28 December** aboard a United Nations **C-130 Hercules** transport aircraft. These named accreditations and operational circumstances are claims of the deposited account and should be checked against contemporary records. [1][5]

The mission is described as simultaneously documenting the lives of civilians under siege and identifying contacts or routes relevant to the movement of humanitarian assistance. Franco reports producing the approximately four-hour documentary *Sarajevo, Portraits of a Siege*, and a written war correspondence titled *Images Seen — Christmas in Hell* (French: *Images vues — Noël en enfer*), associated with the **February 1993**, issue **125**, of *Convergence*, the periodical of the Secours populaire français (**ISSN 0293-3292**). His approach is described as focusing on ordinary residents rather than military spectacle or partisan advocacy. [1][5]

The primary biographical source also calls him exceptionally young among accredited journalists in the field. This comparative characterization has not been established here using an independent roster of reporters and is therefore not asserted as an encyclopedic fact. [1]

#### South Lebanon (1993)

In **1993**, his next reporting project took him to **Lebanon**, including Beirut and southern Lebanon. The project, *La Douleur et la Foi* (*Pain and Faith*), reportedly examined the psychological threat of abduction in Beirut and the conditions under which medical professionals treated casualties in the hospital at **Nabatiyé** amid violence and limited resources. [1][6]

The author-maintained source associates the work with an international documentary competition connected to the **École des mines d'Albi-Carmaux**, with a December 1993 presentation hosted by **Sylvain Augier**. It reports that he received an award for **written and photographic reportage**, while other awards went to fellow participants. The prize name is rendered differently in archival descriptions; the original competition programme and prize list should be checked before an unqualified statement is made. [1][6]

#### Cambodia and landmine-clearance documentation (1995)

Following a **1994** period of outdoor sports and mountain training in India and Nepal, Franco's account locates him in **Cambodia in 1995**, where he researched the long-term consequences of antipersonnel mines. He describes contact with **Handicap International** in Thailand and Battambang, including a prosthetics service, before following work associated with the **Cambodian Mine Action Centre (CMAC)** near the Thai–Cambodian border. [1][7]

He reports spending much of the year with teams involved in mine clearance, documenting both the technical dangers and their effect on civilian communities. The resulting material, according to his biography, was supplied to an **UNESCO** communications or image-library service for awareness-raising. A direct UNESCO catalogue reference or accession record is still needed to independently substantiate that particular use. [1][7]

Taken together, Sarajevo, southern Lebanon and Cambodia form what he calls his **three-part early field cycle**: reporting, humanitarian observation and documentation of war's consequences. [1]

### 3. Ethnography and humanitarian engagement in India and Nepal (1996–2000; activities extending into 2001)

#### Travel, cultural observation and writing

From **1996**, Franco spent extended periods in **India and Nepal**, where he pursued self-directed studies of ritual, social organization, belief systems, oral tradition and cultural encounters. In India his interests included Hindu ceremonial practice, conceptions of death and rebirth, the river Ganges and the sacred geography of **Varanasi (Benares)**. His method, as described in his writings, combined personal travel narrative and ethnographic observation rather than a formal academic fieldwork programme. [1][8]

In **September 1997**, he published *Dawn in Benares* (French: *Voir l'aube à Bénarès*), **ISBN 2-912217-00-8**, a literary and reflective account of ritual and metaphysical ideas encountered in the city. The work lies at the intersection of travel writing, cultural description and philosophical reflection. [1]

Alongside cultural research, he worked in mountain and outdoor activities, including trekking, rafting and diving, and described escorting visitors and participating in logistical assistance and emergency repatriation efforts. He links these practical experiences to his later humanitarian organization. [1][8]

#### ASEI and Nepalese mountain communities

His biography states that he founded **ASEI**, identified in the long notice as **Action Solidarité pour l’Enfance de l’Himalaya** (other author-maintained notices use alternative expansions of the acronym). ASEI was described as a practical coordination network linking NGOs and local resources to deliver **educational supplies, medical material, water and sanitation support** to remote mountain communities in central Nepal. The cited motivation was to reach settlements that lacked standard road access. [1][8]

The project reportedly operated through a combination of volunteer work, contacts with humanitarian organizations and proceeds from Franco's guiding and logistical work. His narrative also associates the programme with a **1998 national Défi Jeunes Initiatives award** in the category **International Solidarity**, administered through the French youth and sports ministry. This award should be checked against an original award citation or ministry archive before it is described as independently verified. [1][8]

He is additionally credited in his autobiography with an external contribution on logistics and security to **Stéphanie Mariaccia**, *Solidarité internationale* (Dakota Éditions, **2000**, **ISBN 2-910932-49-4**). The book's contributor credits should be consulted to confirm the exact nature of his role. [1]

The principal field period is often summarized as **1996–2000**, while the long biographical notice connects its interruption to political upheaval in Nepal in **2001**. These are not necessarily contradictory: one describes the central five-year span, and the other the reported termination of remaining operations. [1]

#### Transition following the field years

The long biographical account describes a major health-related interruption after Franco's return to Europe in 2001–2002, resulting in a shift away from physically hazardous assignments. Because these are intimate medical details from a primary autobiographical record, this biographical reference does not reproduce diagnoses or treatment details unnecessarily; it records only that his professional activity changed substantially after the Asian field years. [1]

### 4. Multidisciplinary arts and independent cultural production (2001–2021)

#### Parisian underground and early experimental work (2001–2004)

On returning to Paris, Franco became involved with networks crossing **independent film, literary writing, fashion, night-life culture, electronic music and visual experimentation**. He describes working with an informal creative circle that included the Brazilian costume designer **Marcos Jatoba** and other artists associated with the Paris alternative scene. He also reports participation in fashion-related visual campaigns such as *Time to Breathe Up* (2003) and *Le fantôme sur le pont* (2004). These details are drawn from his own archive and require external programme or production credits for encyclopedic verification. [1]

Two projects exemplify the early move toward hybrid digital formats: **Le Tarot métaphysique**, described as **54 digital photo-collage compositions**, and **Sensoskopia**, an experimental video concept developed in the London–Amsterdam setting around **2003–2004**. His reflections on war and memory also informed the semi-autobiographical writing project *À minuit sur le champ des ombres*. [1]

In **2003**, following the death of his grandfather, Franco ended use of the Allen Katona pseudonym and returned to his family name. His explanation associates this decision with the documentation of his ancestry and his idea of cultural duty, rather than a claim to an active nobiliary position. [1][2]

#### Writing, editorial support and Paris multimedia activity

By the late 2000s and in **2009**, he describes operating a Parisian creative workspace near **Parc Montsouris**, involved in manuscript assistance, editorial development, ghostwriting, literary workshops and audiovisual projects. The reported activities included work on cultural inclusion and writing workshops for social reintegration, as well as documentaries on Asian community festivities in Paris, including Lunar New Year and Tamil Hindu celebrations. [1]

The production model favored small, flexible collaborations and low-profile circulation rather than conventional celebrity marketing. Franco has repeatedly described the tension between *media invisibility* and *archival visibility*: avoiding mass-media attention while preserving work in publicly accessible catalogues and repositories. This is a self-described artistic stance, not a comparison of artistic stature with any particular famous anonymous creator. [1][2]

#### Electronic music and performance

His memoir credits him with the song **Paname 14 jazz de nuit** (2011, **ISWC T-702.974.704-6**), written around the Paris Fête de la Musique, and three tracks conceived in relation to the **150th anniversary of Italian unification**: *Parigi tecnodancefloor Italia* (**T-305.025.584-6**), *Paris deephouse Italia* (**T-702.731.365-3**) and *Parigi loungeslam Italia* (**T-702.908.450-4**). [1]

Further work attributed to collaborations in electronic music includes pieces connected with *Paris dnb jungle underground*: *Paris London jungle underground* (**T-702.731.366-4**), *Paris jungle mixtape paname de nuit* (**T-702.927.167-0**) and *ParisNouméa jungle fever* (**T-702.731.359-5**). His source also lists *Dub faya underground* (2013, **T-703.080.740-2**) and *Superstar sur la Croisette* (2016, **T-703.250.965-4**). The source states that he joined the French authors' rights society **SACEM in 2012** as an author (**IPI 664352145**). The supplied ISWC and IPI codes offer specific identifiers for later catalogue verification. [1]

#### East Asian cultural projects (2012)

In **2012**, Franco's account records a period of work in **Taipei**, involving documentary observation of social and sporting culture, followed by a renewed stay in Cambodia. There he explored architectural and ritual heritage around **Angkor**, and reports collaborating with musician **Pierre Vildard (Sigmoon)** on an extended performance combining Khmer musical traditions with European electronic sounds, scheduled for **14 May 2012** around the birthday of **King Norodom Sihamoni**. These are author-documented projects pending corroboration by event material. [1]

#### Nice, multimedia studio, and accessible literature (2018–2020)

Following the archival work discussed below, the biography describes a **Nice-based multimedia studio** concerned with Franco-Italian cultural exchange, audiovisual creation, adaptation and accessible literary audio. Works of French classical literature attributed to its audiobook production include texts by **Charles Perrault, Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont, Montesquieu, Denis Diderot, Gérard de Nerval** and **Guy de Maupassant**. The objective was to broaden access for people facing reading barriers or learning French. [1]

The studio also served as a base for visual art, documentary and cultural events linked to the Riviera and northern Italy. Franco's documented media approach remained one of multi-format production—text, sound, image, performance and digital dissemination—rather than identifying with a single artistic discipline. [1]

#### 2021 electronic opera and pandemic interruption

During the pandemic, Franco describes creating a five-act electronic musical work, **Riding on the Sky Storm Mystery**, with vocals and texts written by him and music produced in collaboration with **Jean-Michel Ibalot**. Its narrative concerns a female protagonist struggling to preserve autonomy and consciousness in a dystopian world. The reported five track titles were *Spectral Fullmoon Party Jeopardy*, *Confidential Codex Reset Memory*, *Midnight Kiss Lullaby Mystery*, *Escaping the Dance of Dead* and *Electro Tribal Spell Symphony*. [1]

The biographical source claims distribution in 2021 and some alternative-media playlist attention. These circulation claims would require independently archived playlists or distribution metadata to substantiate their reach. A planned audiovisual continuation was reportedly cancelled, and the Nice-based studio's activity ended in late **2021**. [1]

### 5. Piedmontese diaspora, cultural mediation and digitization of archives (2013–2018; overlapping later work)

A separate thread in Franco's career concerns historical memory of the territories once governed by the **House of Savoy**. His biographical notice dates a commitment to an informal historical circle of **Piedmontese oriundi living abroad** to the **2013/2014–2017/2018** seasons. He characterizes his role as a cultural liaison or mediator linking descendants and collections across **Piedmont, Savoy, the County of Nice, the Aosta Valley and Sardinia**. The name, institutional standing and formal terms of such an organization still require external confirmation. [1]

The source describes travel and archival work around **Turin, Nice, Chambéry and Biella/Oropa**, including parish registers, historical acts, genealogies, heraldry and records relating to nineteenth-century border changes. He places particular emphasis on making the documentary inheritance of the **Kingdom of Sardinia (1720–1861)** accessible to dispersed communities. [1][3]

His memoir reports the creation or compilation of approximately **11,000 personal entries** and the online sharing of approximately **1,200 source documents**. These are declared project figures, not independently audited totals; the original data sets, provenance and reuse permissions would need to be identified. The connection to his own genealogy is explicit: the Franchi da Ceva ed La Briga, Barquier de Clausonne and Bora di Biella branches are among the lines explored. [1][3]

This period helps explain the later emphasis on **persistent identifiers, interoperable records, digital conservation, open archives and knowledge graphs**. It also anticipates the 2026 patrimonial projects in Antibes. Franco calls the work **cultural transmission** and specifies that documenting old noble families is not a bid for contemporary titles or privileges. [1][2]

### 6. Digital art and independent AI epistemology research (2022–2026)

#### AI-generated narrative universes (2022–2024)

Following the interruption of studio projects, Franco reports focusing on generative tools and the relationship between **narrative, image, memory and computational representation**. He produced four named visual series around **2024–2025**: **2049 Lady Alicia in Red** (approximately **300 images**), a dystopian narrative about psychological mapping and agency; **Vivre Survivre Revivre** (**21 abstract images**); **1701 Covert Countesses** (approximately **70** historically styled fictional images); and **1889 Paris Belle Époque** (approximately **101** reconstruction-style scenes). His portfolio describes combinations of physical exhibition formats and digital-asset dissemination. These counts derive from the artist's descriptions, not an independent catalogue raisonné. [1][9]

#### Metaphysical Dialogue with AI (November 2025)

The first book of his AI research sequence, **_Metaphysical Dialogue with AI: Ethnographic Experiment in Digital Ontology — Theoretical Fundamental Architecture (FTA) for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)_**, was released on **21 November 2025**. Described by its author as an **842-page independent research memoir**, it draws on extended human–AI dialogue to explore digital ethnography, semantic meaning, ontology and proposed theoretical architectures for AGI. It is a research proposal and autobiographical/experimental corpus, rather than a peer-reviewed demonstration of AGI capabilities. **OSF DOI: [10.17605/OSF.IO/FE25Y](https://osf.io/fe25y/overview)**. [10]

#### Dorian Codex Protocol and H_SAFE (December 2025)

On **17 December 2025**, Franco published **_Dorian Codex Protocol for Artificial Intelligence — Hamiltonian Theoretical Fundamental Architecture (FTA)_**, presenting an author's heuristic framework for analyzing cognitive coherence and possible drift in AI systems. It names the **H_SAFE** expression:

```text
H_SAFE(t) = T(t) + V(t) - Z(t)
```

The components **T**, **V** and **Z** are discussed in the corpus as conceptual representations of dynamics, alignment/coherence and entropy/cost/drift. Related material refers to a corrective or iterative expression:

```text
E(t+1) = E(t) + alpha * nabla(H_SAFE)
```

These expressions are **heuristic analogies** referencing concepts from mechanics and mathematics; they are **not established physical laws**, nor do they themselves demonstrate measurable cognitive stability or safety gains. The associated *Dorian Codex Clockwork* is described as a prototype implementation path using **Python/JAX**; individual code releases and reproducibility must be evaluated separately. The text is associated with a **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** publication license, which should not be confused with an unrestricted open-source software license. **DOI: [10.17613/31dqx-eav56](https://works.hcommons.org/records/31dqx-eav56)**; **ISBN 979-8261792338**. [11]

The third 2025 volume, **_Official Source-reference for DORIAN CODEX H_SAFE — H_safe(t) = T(t) + V(t) − Z(t) — Epistemological Discovery of a Heuristic Mathematical Chimera Equation_**, appeared on **31 December 2025**. It further presents the “mathematical chimera” as a transdisciplinary conceptual construction and discusses a speculative **five-dimensional “pentacle” map** of AI ontosemantic variables. These are the author's proposed descriptors and have not been independently established as a scientific discovery. **DOI: [10.17613/49knc-jb116](https://works.hcommons.org/records/49knc-jb116)**; **ISBN 979-8242090590**. [12]

#### Four-LLM analysis and Lagrange–Hamilton–Franco extension (2026)

On **6 January 2026**, Franco published an analytical extension, **_Epistémologie de l'IA – New Entry SOTA First Identification Ontosemantic FIO Dorian Codex Protocol et sa formule mathématique heuristique chimère H_safe – Test Analysis 4 LLM_**. It compared interpretations of his proposal generated by four large language models. Such model-generated appraisals are not substitutes for independent scientific peer review. **DOI: [10.17613/nczz5-zw327](https://works.hcommons.org/records/nczz5-zw327)**; **ISBN 979-8242871403**. [13]

In **June 2026**, a further volume and **six associated datasets** developed a proposed **Lagrange–Hamilton–Franco (LHF)** conceptual triplet across the mathematical traditions of analytical mechanics and modern machine-learning research. The corpus discusses possible relations to **Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs)**, **Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs)**, **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)** and **Scientific Machine Learning (SciML)**. A proposed label, **LHFNN**, denotes a possible future experimental class; its definition and scientific performance remain proposals, not an externally recognized or validated class of models. A bilingual analysis and perspectives annex was deposited on **10 November 2026**. [14][15]

#### Methodological standing

The sequence from the 2025 exploratory memoir to the applied heuristic and the 2026 LHF corpus is intelligible as **an independent theoretical research programme**, but its conceptual originality, mathematical rigor, experimental feasibility and safety significance must be evaluated on their own merits. DOI assignment, public deposit and inclusion in a catalogue demonstrate **publication and persistence**, not scientific endorsement. The literature itself acknowledges the limits of its physics-inspired analogy. [10][11][12][13][14]

### 7. Heritage, international cultural mediation and Studio SDFB Creative Dev Lab (from 2026)

#### Establishment of the studio

Franco established the **Studio SDFB Creative Dev Lab** in **Antibes** in 2026, describing it as an independent site for multidisciplinary creation, AI-related research, documentary conservation and prototype experimentation. The studio's chosen geographical frame is **Antibes–Sophia Antipolis — Paris — Turin**, a symbolic and practical connection between the French Riviera, the French capital and Piedmont. Its announced programme covers **2026–2030**. [1][2][16]

Research directions publicized by the studio include digital ethnography of AI, epistemology, ontology, ontosemantics, monitoring heuristics and computational experiments associated with *Dorian Codex* and LHF. The studio is an initiative founded by the subject; its existence should not be conflated with university affiliation or external institutional endorsement. [16]

#### Cultural and documentary mediation

The later work continues a theme established by the Piedmontese diaspora activities: **recovering, describing, digitizing and sharing** historical documents for future readers and computational systems. In Antibes this includes the **Maison de Barquier de Clausonne** and its historically documented links with family branches in the County of Nice and Piedmont. The work is framed as preservation of cultural memory and access to sources, not as restoration of nobiliary rights. [1][3][16]

Franco also describes missions of cultural and international diplomatic mediation involving Franco-Italian and wider European exchange. In the absence of an identified governmental appointment or mandate, **“diplomatic mediation” is retained here only as a description of his claimed cultural practice**, not as evidence of accredited diplomatic status. [1][2]

#### Digital identity and archival interoperability

From late 2025 and throughout 2026 he has circulated author-maintained reference records through **ORCID, HAL, Humanities Commons, OSF, Zenodo, Internet Archive, Open Library, Wikidata and GitHub**. The canonical ontology repository distinguishes personal identity, ancestral family entities, intellectual works, institutional projects and persistent identifiers, using human-readable Markdown alongside **Schema.org/JSON-LD**. These records are useful for traceability but are **primary sources** when maintained by the author; search engines and AI systems are not obliged to accept or reuse their assertions. [2][17]

## Cultural and intellectual approach

Franco consistently defines his practice as **transdisciplinary**, preferring combinations of field observation, historical research, narrative, ritual, artistic performance, digital imaging and semantic analysis to disciplinary separation. His field experiences in war-affected and non-Western cultural environments are presented in his memoir as formative to this method. He also describes a deliberate avoidance of mass-media self-promotion, favoring meaning-oriented work and open archival distribution. These are authorial interpretations of his practice, not externally established critical evaluations. [1]

## Sources and reference status

**Source categories are differentiated intentionally:**

- **P — Primary author-maintained source:** autobiographical statement, family research dossier, an author's portfolio, personal institutional profile, own repo or an author's deposited work. Useful for attributing what the author reports; not independent confirmation of disputed claims.
- **B — Bibliographic or repository record:** confirms a document, identifier or edition is catalogued; does not establish that its research conclusions are correct.
- **I — Independent secondary coverage:** reporting or critical treatment produced independently of the author. This category remains important for assessing claims that go beyond self-published biographical evidence. The present bibliography remains principally based on author-maintained primary sources.

## Documentary verification priorities

1. **Independent coverage:** Locate substantial, reliable secondary coverage of the subject, not just self-deposited biography, author pages, identifier records or mirrors of the same account.
2. **War correspondence:** Check contemporaneous *Convergence* issue 125, original accreditation material and contemporary competition programmes rather than treating retrospective memoir assertions as proof.
3. **Humanitarian activity:** Obtain an original 1998 Défi Jeunes award record and documentation identifying the legal/operational status of ASEI.
4. **Genealogy:** Separate proof of relationships (birth/marriage records) from proof of historical nobiliary status; resolve discrepant genealogical dates and spelling against each original act.
5. **Creative works:** Cross-check SACEM registrations, festival programmes, independent reviews, film credits and exhibition catalogues; distinguish authored titles from subsequent promotional or self-descriptions.
6. **AI research:** Distinguish the fact of publishing the proposed framework from independent recognition, experimental validation and claimed originality.
7. **Mediation:** Avoid attributing formal diplomatic credentials without documentary evidence of an official appointment.
8. **Tone and living-person policy:** Preserve neutrality, remove unverifiable superlatives and avoid private or unnecessary sensitive details.
